01
Quantum gates

How do qubits change without losing information?

Rule 1: gates can be undone

A classical AND gate loses information: an output of 0 could come from 00, 01 or 10, so you can't go back.

A quantum gate never does: n qubits in, n qubits out, and you can always run it backwards. The one step you can't undo is measurement.

In math terms, gates are “unitary” matrices (more on slide 9).

Flip the bit, flip the sign

X gate

Quantum NOT: swaps |0⟩ and |1⟩.

Z gate

Sign flip: |1⟩ gets a minus sign, |0⟩ is untouched.

Y gate

Does both at once: flips the bit and the sign.

The minus sign is invisible at measurement, but it decides what cancels later.

Hadamard: the coin flip

H moves |0⟩ from the north pole to the equator: a 50/50 superposition.

50 / 50

H turns a certain |0⟩ into an equal superposition

H, then H

brings |0⟩ back for certain (a real coin flipped twice stays random!)

Almost every algorithm starts with H on every qubit: all inputs at once, equal weight.

CNOT links two qubits

If the first qubit is 1, flip the second.

InputOutputWhat happens
0000First is 0: nothing happens
0101First is 0: nothing happens
1011First is 1: flip the second
1110First is 1: flip the second

Give it a 50/50 first qubit and the pair is linked: (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2. Measure one, and you know the other.

The math behind gates

Source: Nielsen & Chuang (2010), Fig. 4.2.

  • Gates are unitary: U†U = I, so probability is preserved and U−1 = U†
  • CNOT: |c, t⟩ → |c, t ⊕ c⟩
  • {H, S, T, CNOT} approximates any operation to any accuracy (S = T²)
  • Solovay–Kitaev: single-qubit gates need only O(logc(1/ε)) gates, c ≈ 2; arbitrary n-qubit operations can still need exponentially many

The catch

Measuring gives back one random answer. Parallelism alone is not the trick.

So what is the trick? Interference.

Make wrong answers cancel

2 qubits, looking for 11. The amplitude of each answer at each stage.

100%

on 11 after one amplify step

Amplitudes can be negative, so they add up or cancel, like waves. Not “try everything at once”: engineered cancellation.

One check, not 524,289

A hidden function is either “always the same” or “half 0, half 1”. Which one?

MethodChecksAnswer
Classical524,289Up to this many, to be certain (20-bit inputs)
Quantum1Interference gives “all zeros” (always the same) or “never all zeros” (half and half)

A teaching example, not a practical speedup: random classical checks are nearly always right.

Grover's search

N items, no order, no index, one is special. Find it.

Grover: √N instead of N

Steps needed as the list grows (log-log). Classical N/2, Grover about 0.8 × √N.

500,000

checks for 1,000,000 items, classical

≈ 785

Grover steps for the same list

Proven best possible for this problem. A big speedup, but not exponential.

Each round: mark, then amplify

Mark (oracle)A checker puts a minus sign on the answer, without saying which one.
Amplify (diffusion)Flip every amplitude around the average: the marked one jumps up, the rest shrink.

Rotate, and stop in time

Source: Nielsen & Chuang (2010), Fig. 6.3. The book starts at θ/2 and rotates by θ; we say θ and 2θ.

0.8 × √N

rounds is about the best place to stop

94.5% → 33% → 1%

for 8 items after 2, 3 and 4 rounds: too many rounds overshoot

You don't need the answer to know when to stop, only how many items there are.

How many rounds?

Best number of rounds is about (π/4)√N. Success after k rounds is sin²((2k+1)θ).

The Grover formulas

The numbers behind the search:

  • Start angle θ with sin θ = 1/√N
  • Each round turns the state by 2θ
  • Diffusion reflects about the mean: a → 2·mean − a
  • 3 qubits, amplitude of 101: 0.354 → 0.884 → 0.972
  • No quantum algorithm beats order √N (Bennett et al., 1997)

Shor & crypto

Why factoring matters for security

Factoring = finding a rhythm

Powers of 7, remainder after dividing by 15. The pattern repeats every 4 steps.

7, 4, 13, 1

remainders of powers of 7 divided by 15, then it repeats

15 = 3 × 5

the period 4 hands you the factors

For a 2,048-bit number the pattern is astronomically long.

The QFT: a tuner for rhythms

Shor by the numbers

01
Period

f(x) = ax mod N repeats with period r.

Conditions

r even and ar/2 not ≡ −1 (mod N): true for at least half of random a.

gcd(ar/2 ± 1, N). For 15: gcd(48, 15) = 3, gcd(50, 15) = 5.
